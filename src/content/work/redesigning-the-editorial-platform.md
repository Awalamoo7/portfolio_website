When we inherited the editorial platform, it was a decade-old WordPress installation held together with custom plugins and institutional memory. The writing team had developed an elaborate system of workarounds — Google Docs for drafts, Slack for approvals, a shared spreadsheet for the editorial calendar. The CMS was, in practice, just the last step: copy-paste and publish.

We needed to change that without breaking the team's momentum.

## Understanding the Problem

The first thing we did was not write any code. Instead, we spent three weeks embedded with the editorial team, watching how they worked. We sat in pitch meetings, observed the editing process, tracked a story from idea to publication.

What we found was that the CMS wasn't just outdated — it was actively hostile to the way the team worked. The publishing workflow assumed a linear process (write, edit, publish) when the reality was iterative and collaborative. Stories went through multiple rounds of feedback, often from different editors at different stages.

## The Approach

We built the new platform around three principles:

1. **Collaboration is the default.** Every document is multiplayer from the start. Comments, suggestions, and edits happen in-place, not in a separate tool.

2. **The calendar is the source of truth.** Instead of a separate spreadsheet, the editorial calendar is integrated into the platform. Assigning a story to a date creates the draft automatically.

3. **Publishing is a button, not a process.** Once a story is approved, publishing takes one click. No copy-pasting, no formatting fixes, no broken images.

## Results

Six months after launch, the team was publishing 30% more stories with the same headcount. More importantly, the workarounds disappeared. The platform became the place where work happened, not just where it ended up.

The lesson: sometimes the best technology investment isn't a new feature — it's removing the friction that makes people route around your tools entirely.
